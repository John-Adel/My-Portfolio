About

I am a computer science undergraduate at Zagazig National University, where my coursework sits inside the university's Medical Informatics track. That academic label undersells what I actually do day to day: build and test predictive models the same way whether the columns in front of me describe patients, customers, or transactions. The math underneath doesn't change — only the domain does, and I've set out to prove that versatility rather than assume it.

Outside the classroom, I edit video for 180 Daraga's media team at Cairo University, cutting reels and event coverage in Adobe Premiere Pro and After Effects. That habit carries into my data work: when a model or a pipeline is finished, I don't stop at a notebook — I build the explainer that makes the result legible to someone who has never opened Jupyter.

I am currently training as a Machine Learning Trainee with the Digital Egypt Pioneers Initiative, under Egypt's Ministry of Communications and Information Technology, and have delivered freelance data and video work on international platforms alongside my degree.

Services

Custom Machine Learning & Predictive Modeling

Turning historical data into models that predict what happens next — classification, regression, or forecasting, built and validated the way the problem actually needs, not just to hit a metric.

  • Feature engineering and model selection with scikit-learn
  • Train/test validation with clear performance reporting
  • Write-ups that translate model output into a decision

Data Cleaning, Wrangling & Exploratory Analysis

Before a dataset can support any decision, it usually needs work — missing values, inconsistent formats, and outliers cleaned up, then explored until the shape of the problem is actually visible.

  • Structured cleaning pipelines in Pandas and NumPy
  • Exploratory analysis with Seaborn, Matplotlib, and Plotly
  • Summary reporting suited to non-technical stakeholders

Guarantee

A few commitments that apply to every project, not just the ones with a client watching.

Reproducible by default

Every analysis lives in a notebook or script someone else can re-run, with the same libraries and the same steps — the result shouldn't depend on remembering what order I clicked things in.

Careful with the data itself

No dataset gets used beyond what its source or its subjects agreed to, and nothing personal or sensitive is assumed fair game without explicit permission.

Visual transparency

If a chart or model claims something, the reasoning behind it is shown, not just the headline number — so the person reading it can trust the conclusion, not just take it on faith.
